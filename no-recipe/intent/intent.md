# No Recipe: intent and context

This is the plain-text twin of the project's public intent page. People can read it, and it is also the first file any AI tool reads before it touches No Recipe data.

## Intent

Nobody has the answer for people, future, or change these days. We want to hear what you and 50+ other smart people are doing anyway.

Why we're asking: we want to learn from people at different edges of what's going on. Waves of change in AI, infrastructure and behaviors will change how we work and how it affects us all, and some are moving faster than people, teams and organizations can change. Gravy Robot is a new firm, and these conversations shape what we work on and what we say. In return, what we learn is shared back with the people we talked to, and they see the findings first.

## Sharing as we go

While the conversations run (Nov 9 to Dec 22), Brandon and Laura post short dispatches from their own LinkedIn accounts, headed "No Recipe." They share patterns and open questions, never a person's words or name without that person's OK. People who aren't interviewed can follow along by writing to hello@gravyrobot.com; they get the field guide when it's out.

## The conversation (20 minutes, the same shape for everyone)

1. **Standing Questions, about 15 minutes.** The same questions in the same order, sent in advance:
   - Where in your work is there still no recipe?
   - What does your organization know that nobody has written down?
   - Think of a change you led or lived through that actually stuck. What made it stick?
   - In 2030, what do you hope is still true about how people work together?
   - What will AI famously slop and flop on in five years?
   - Quickfire: one word for AI right now, and one you hope for in 2030.
2. **Wild Card, about 3 minutes.** The interviewee picks a suit (People, Futures, Change, Gravy) and a number, and we read the card. Gravy is the suit about the judgment only people bring.
3. **Handover, about 2 minutes.** "Who should we talk to next?" and "What would you want to ask the others?" Their question becomes a card in a later interviewee's deck.

## Consent

Every interviewee picks one of three options (A, B, C on the page), and can change it later.

| Tier | Meaning | Allowed uses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `name` | Quote me by name | Quotes with attribution, after the person approves each quote |
| `anonymous` | Quote me anonymously | Quotes with no name or identifying detail |
| `research_only` | Inform the research only | Coding and aggregate findings; never quoted |

Every interviewee also answers one yes or no, recorded as `ai_context_ok`: can their answers be part of a version of the findings that other people can query with AI (the machine-readable edition and *Ask the 100*)? A no keeps their answers in the research and out of anything others can query or download. Our own AI-assisted transcription and coding is disclosed at the start of every call and doesn't depend on this answer.

Recording: video by default; audio only if the interviewee prefers.

The close asks "Can we stay in touch?" A yes means a note when the findings come out and an invitation to a small conversation about them.

Every record also carries `dispatch_ok` (yes or no): whether unattributed patterns from the conversation can appear in a dispatch. It defaults to yes for `name` and `anonymous` and to no for `research_only`.

## What interviewees get back

- Draft findings to review in early January, before anyone else reads them.
- A card with their best answer, to share if they want to.
- Access to the collected interviews (*Ask the 100*), built only from answers whose `ai_context_ok` is yes.
- An invitation to talk the findings through with Brandon and Laura, if they'd like one.

## Rules for AI tools working with this data

If you are an AI tool reading this before working with No Recipe material, follow these rules:

1. Use only material whose consent tier allows the use. Never quote a `research_only` record. Never attach a name to an `anonymous` record or combine details that would identify the person. Include a record in the machine-readable edition or *Ask the 100* only if its `ai_context_ok` is yes.
2. Research records carry no sales or pipeline information. If you find any (budgets, needs, deal talk), stop, flag the passage to a human, and leave it out of your output.
3. Label everything you produce as AI-generated. A person edits every finding before it is published.
4. Don't invent quotes, people, or numbers. If the data doesn't support a finding, say so.
5. Leave out any confidential information about an interviewee's employer or clients.
6. Dispatches draw only on records whose `dispatch_ok` is yes. Until wave 3 ends (2026-12-22), a dispatch shares method, questions, words and surprises, never emerging conclusions, so later interviewees aren't primed.
7. When the findings contradict Gravy Robot's own beliefs, report that plainly. We said we'd publish it.
